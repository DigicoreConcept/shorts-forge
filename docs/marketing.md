# Excido — Marketing & User Acquisition Strategy
**Product:** Excido (AI video clipping SaaS)
**Company:** Digicoreconcept Limited
**Status:** Draft v1 — self-funded, pre-launch/beta stage

---

## 1. Strategic Overview

Excido's acquisition strategy is built on **proof before pitch**: rather than marketing to creators with no track record, the founder uses the product personally first, generates real results, and uses that evidence to convert creators one at a time. This is deliberately a long-game, trust-based approach rather than a paid-acquisition or viral-growth play — appropriate for a bootstrapped, pre-revenue product with no marketing budget and no existing audience.

A secondary, lower-effort channel (cold outreach) runs in parallel as a test, not a primary bet.

---

## 2. Phase 1 — Personal Use & Proof of Concept

**What:** Run the founder's own YouTube channel's long-form content through Excido to generate short-form clips.

**Purpose:** Before asking any creator to trust an unproven tool, generate first-hand, verifiable results — real clips, real output quality, real time saved.

**Actions:**
- Process a consistent batch of the founder's own videos through Excido over a defined period (recommend minimum 4–6 weeks / 10+ videos before drawing conclusions — a single video isn't a sample size).
- Track output quality subjectively (does the clip selection actually capture the best moments?) and objectively (time from upload to finished clip, number of usable clips per video).
- Post the resulting clips on the founder's own channels/socials to see real audience reception — views, engagement, retention — not just internal quality judgment.

**Output of this phase:** A documented before/after — "here's what manual clipping used to take me, here's what Excido produced, here's how those clips performed publicly."

---

## 3. Phase 2 — Analysis

**What:** Review Phase 1 results honestly before using them as a pitch.

**Actions:**
- Compare time spent (manual clipping process, if any baseline exists) vs. time spent using Excido.
- Compare clip performance (views/engagement) of Excido-assisted clips vs. any prior manually-made clips, if a baseline exists.
- Identify weak points in the output honestly — where did the tool pick a bad moment, miss context, or need manual correction? This matters more for the product roadmap than for marketing, but it also determines what claims can honestly be made to creators later.
- Decide, based on real data, whether the results are strong enough to be used as a pitch asset yet, or whether another cycle of use/refinement is needed first.

**Gate:** Don't proceed to Phase 3 outreach until there's a real, defensible result to point to. A weak or unconvincing personal case study undermines the entire "proof before pitch" premise — it's better to extend Phase 1 than to pitch prematurely on thin evidence.

---

## 4. Phase 3 — Gradual Creator Conversion ("The Long Game")

**What:** Use the Phase 1/2 proof to convince other content creators to try Excido, incrementally, one relationship at a time — not a mass campaign.

**Actions:**
- Identify a small initial target list of creators the founder has some existing connection or reasonable access to (not cold strangers) — this phase relies on relationship-based trust, not broad reach.
- Approach with the actual case study: real before/after, real numbers, real clips — not a generic pitch.
- Offer early access / free usage in exchange for feedback and (ideally) permission to use their results as a further case study — this compounds the proof over time, each successful creator becomes the next pitch asset.
- Expect slow, deliberate growth — this is explicitly a long-game channel, not a fast-scale one. Success is measured in creators actually retained and using the product, not signups.

**Compounding effect:** Each creator who succeeds with Excido becomes a stronger, more diverse proof point than the founder's own channel alone — over time this shifts the pitch from "I built this and it works for me" to "here's what it's done for creators like you."

---

## 5. Phase 4 — Cold Messaging (Parallel, Secondary Channel)

**What:** Direct cold outreach to content creators, run alongside Phases 1–3, treated as a lower-confidence test rather than a primary channel.

**Actions:**
- Draft a short, personal (not templated-sounding) message referencing the creator's actual content, similar in spirit to the organizer outreach approach already used for Eventty.
- Test in small batches (10–15 messages at a time) rather than mass-sending, to gauge response rate before scaling effort into it.
- Track response rate and conversion separately from Phase 3 — this is explicitly being run "in case it works," not because it's expected to be the primary channel.

**Honest expectation to set:** Cold outreach to creators typically converts lower than warm/relationship-based approaches, especially with no case study to point to yet. This channel should lean on Phase 1/2 proof once available, rather than running purely cold.

---

## 6. Channels Not Currently in Scope

For documentation completeness — these were identified as possible acquisition mechanics but are **not part of the current strategy**, and are noted here so they can be revisited later rather than forgotten:

- **SEO** — no content/keyword strategy currently planned.
- **Affiliate program for editors** — not currently planned; could be a Phase 3+ extension once there's a base of successful creators to draw affiliates from.
- **Product-led growth (PLG) mechanics** — e.g., a "Made with Excido" watermark on free-tier exports. Not currently planned but is a low-cost, passive acquisition lever worth revisiting once there's a free tier live, since it costs nothing per-use and scales with actual product usage.

---

## 7. Success Metrics

| Phase | Primary Metric | Target Signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Personal use | Time saved per video, clip usability | Consistent usable clips across 10+ videos |
| 2 — Analysis | Honest go/no-go decision | Defensible before/after case study exists |
| 3 — Creator conversion | Creators retained & actively using | Each new creator becomes a usable case study |
| 4 — Cold messaging | Response rate | Track separately; not expected to outperform Phase 3 |

---

## 8. Open Items / Not Yet Decided

- No defined timeline/deadline for how long Phase 1 runs before moving to Phase 3 — recommend setting one (e.g., 6 weeks) rather than leaving it open-ended.
- No target number of Phase 3 creators defined for an initial cohort (recommend starting with 3–5, matching the same "small early cohort" approach used for Eventty's beta).
- SEO, affiliate, and PLG channels are deliberately deferred, not abandoned — revisit once Phase 3 produces real case studies.
